My grandmother had a coat she had mended so many times that the mending became the coat. You could see the stitches. Different threads from different years, some careful, some done in a hurry before work. Machines could make a coat with no visible seams at all, perfect and cheap, and she owned some of those too. But the mended one was the one she loved, and now that she is gone it is the one we kept. Not despite the visible work. Because of it.

I think about that coat when people ask me, with real worry in their voices, what is left for a person to do when the machine can do most of the work. It is a fair fear. More and more of what fills our screens is generated. Text, images, whole reports, produced in seconds, clean and confident and endless. If a machine can make it, they ask, why would mine matter.

Here is what I have come to believe. When output becomes cheap and abundant, the thing that becomes precious is not more output. It is the signs that a human actually cared.

## Abundance changes what is scarce

Scarcity is what gives a thing value, and abundance moves scarcity around. When photographs were hard to make, a sharp, correct photograph was impressive on its own. Now everyone carries a camera that makes technically perfect images all day, and a technically perfect image impresses no one. What moved us instead? The photograph that shows someone was there, at the right moment, that they noticed something the rest of us walked past. The seeing became the scarce part. The machinery became the cheap part.

The same shift is coming for a great deal of work, and it is already here in places. When anyone can generate a polished paragraph, a polished paragraph stops being proof of anything. It no longer tells you the person tried, or knew the subject, or meant it. So the value drains out of the polish and pools somewhere else. It pools in the things a machine cannot fake without a human deciding to make it so. The judgment to cut the clever part that was actually wrong. The honesty to say "I am not sure" instead of generating confidence. The taste to know which of a hundred good options is the right one for this reader, in this moment.

Those are not outputs. They are the fingerprints of a person who gave a damn.

## The value of visible effort goes up, not down

I want to say this plainly, because the gloomy version is so loud right now. When most of the work is machine-made, being visibly human is not a weakness you have to hide. It is the differentiator, and it grows more valuable, not less.

Think about what you trust. If someone hands you a report and you can tell no person read it before it reached you, something in you closes. You start checking it, because nobody stood behind it. Now imagine the same report where someone clearly wrestled with it. They left in a note that says this section is weaker than the rest. They tell you what they checked and what they didn't. You relax, because a human took responsibility. That taking of responsibility has a price, and the price is going up as it gets rarer.

This is not a call to make everything by hand out of romantic stubbornness. I use these tools every day. They do the heavy hauling well. The point is what you do with the time they give back. You can spend it generating more, adding to the flood. Or you can spend it on the parts only a caring person can do. Deciding what is worth making at all. Checking whether it is true. Making sure it lands softly with the person on the other end.

Care is expensive because it costs attention, and attention is the one thing the machines cannot manufacture more of for you. It is yours. It is finite. Where you spend it is the most human signal you can send.

So I am not worried the way I am supposed to be. The coat with the visible stitches is still the one people keep. When the flawless version is everywhere and free, the mended one, the one that shows a person was here and tried, becomes the thing worth having. Be the visible stitches. In a world of frictionless output, honest effort is not old-fashioned. It is the last thing that is genuinely yours to give.
